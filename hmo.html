HMO essentials

The essentials, at a glance.

Requirements depend on your property and your council, so always check the details that apply to you.

Licensing

Larger HMOs need a mandatory licence, and many councils also run additional or selective licensing schemes. Rules differ by council, so check yours before you let.

Fire safety

Working smoke and heat alarms, clear escape routes and, in many HMOs, fire doors and emergency lighting. A fire risk assessment is a sensible starting point.

Gas and electrical safety

A gas safety certificate every year where there is a gas supply, and an electrical installation condition report at least every five years.

Room sizes and facilities

Minimum bedroom sizes, plus enough kitchens, bathrooms and toilets for the number of people living there.

Management duties

HMO managers have extra duties, including keeping shared areas clean and safe, maintaining facilities and giving tenants an emergency contact.

Tenancies and deposits

Protect deposits in a government-approved scheme, give the prescribed information, and carry out right to rent checks.

General guidance for landlords in England, not legal advice. Check the rules that apply to your property with your local council or a qualified adviser.

Before you decide.

Straight answers to what HMO landlords ask us most.

What is an HMO?

In England, a house in multiple occupation (HMO) is generally a property let to three or more people who are not from the same household and who share facilities such as a kitchen or bathroom.

Do I need a licence for my HMO?

Larger HMOs, generally those let to five or more people from more than one household, need a mandatory licence. Many councils also require licences for smaller HMOs. Check with your local council.

Is the management review really free?

Yes. Your management review is free and there is no obligation to instruct us afterwards.
